Understanding Poverty

Poverty is more than just a lack of money. It is a multidimensional challenge affecting every aspect of human life, from education and health to safety and opportunity.

1

Absolute Poverty

A condition characterized by severe deprivation of basic human needs, including food, safe drinking water, sanitation facilities, health, shelter, education and information.

2

Relative Poverty

Defined in relation to the economic status of other members of the society. People are considered poor if they fall below prevailing standards of living in a given societal context.

3

Multidimensional Poverty

Measures poverty beyond income, capturing overlapping deprivations experienced simultaneously, such as poor health, malnutrition, inadequate living standards, and disempowerment.

The Root Causes

Limited Education

Without quality education, finding secure, well-paying employment becomes incredibly difficult.

Unemployment

A lack of available jobs or precarious, informal labor traps families in financial instability.

Systemic Inequality

Historical, social, and economic barriers that marginalize specific communities.

Environmental Pressures

Climate-related disasters disproportionately impact vulnerable populations.

The Consequences

Health & Nutrition

Increased vulnerability to diseases, malnutrition, and reduced life expectancy.

Housing Instability

Living in unsafe or inadequate conditions without basic sanitation.

Social Exclusion

Being marginalized from societal participation and decision-making processes.

Generational Cycle

Disadvantages passed down, making social mobility increasingly difficult.

Global Perspectives

Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives requires us to analyze issues through different lenses. Here is how various stakeholders view the challenge of poverty.

Perspective Primary Focus Advantages of Approach Limitations
Individuals & Families Immediate needs: food, stable income, safe housing, healthcare, and security. Deep, lived understanding of reality; highly motivated for direct improvement. Often lack resources, systemic power, or capital to effect large-scale change.
Governments Economic development, public services, taxation, and social protection programs. Can implement systemic changes, create infrastructure, and alter policy at scale. May be hindered by bureaucracy, political instability, or budget constraints.
NGOs & Communities Targeted community programs, emergency relief, education, and livelihood support. Agile, deeply connected to local needs, and capable of piloting innovative solutions. Reliant on funding/donations; scale is often limited compared to government.
International Orgs (UN, World Bank) Global goals (SDGs), international development assistance, and structural research. Vast resources, global coordination, standardized measurement (e.g., MPI). Can be detached from local nuances; policies may not always fit specific national contexts.

Can Poverty Be Solved Through One Approach Alone?

Our research concludes that a multi-stakeholder approach is essential. Emergency assistance, long-term education, fair economic policy, and community empowerment must work in tandem. No single entity possesses the complete solution to multidimensional poverty.

Poverty in India & Our Local Context

Understanding the unique dynamics of poverty within our nation, and bridging the gap between global understanding and local action.

Rural vs. Urban Realities

Poverty in India presents different challenges depending on geography. While rural poverty is heavily linked to agricultural reliance and access to basic amenities, urban poverty—visible even in developing cities like Gurugram—is often characterized by informal housing, unsecure labor, and strained city infrastructure.

Our Solutions & Areas of Focus

Addressing poverty requires structural change, community support, and continuous education. Here are the primary areas we are researching.

Education & Skills

Supporting access to learning resources, digital literacy, and vocational training to create pathways out of poverty.

Employment & Livelihoods

Examining the need for decent work, fair wages, and sustainable income-generating opportunities.

Healthcare & Nutrition

Understanding the foundational importance of accessible healthcare, clean sanitation, and food security.

Community Partnerships

Exploring responsible volunteering and how to support established, verified community organizations effectively.

Public Policy

Analyzing the role of social protection systems, inclusive infrastructure, and equitable governance.

Awareness Building

Helping our peers and community understand the true causes and complexities of global inequality.

Associated NGO

The Earth Saviours Foundation

As part of our commitment to understanding poverty beyond the classroom, Helping Hands associates with The Earth Saviours Foundation. Based near Gurugram, this internationally recognized NGO is dedicated to rescuing and sheltering abandoned senior citizens, destitute individuals, and victims of severe social marginalization.

By learning from their grassroots initiatives, we gain real-world insights into how community organizations provide dignity, essential healthcare, and safe housing to those most vulnerable to extreme poverty.
